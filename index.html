What it does

One question, answered with evidence

Most audio tools show a number. Frequency Lab shows whether that number holds up — in a steady tone, across a whole track, or on a video someone sent you.

01 — VERIFY

Is this tone at 528 Hz?

Set a target and a tolerance. The verdict counts how many signal frames actually contain a tone-like peak inside that band — over the last 10 seconds and the whole session.

02 — SEE

What else is in here?

A log-frequency spectrum from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, a scrolling spectrogram, and the six strongest peaks with their salience above the noise floor.

03 — PROVE

Can I show someone?

Export the measurements as CSV or a PNG of both plots. The setup travels in the URL, so a link can open the workbench at exactly your target.

Questions

The honest answers

Does it upload my audio?

No. The microphone, tab capture and file decoding all happen in your browser, and there is no upload path in the code. There are no accounts, cookies or third-party scripts on this site.

Is this a guitar tuner?

For steady tones it is precise: ±0.2 Hz on a clean test tone, with note and cents. For chords and full mixes, the loudest peak is not the pitch — see the guide.

Can it tell me what key a song is in?

Not yet. Key and chroma analysis is the next milestone. Today the tool reports spectral peaks, target evidence and band behaviour.

Can it measure 7.83 Hz or 40 Hz?

40 Hz, yes. 7.83 Hz is below the workbench's 20 Hz floor and below what a typical laptop microphone reproduces. The presets page marks those cases instead of pretending.

Does it work on a phone?

The microphone path works over HTTPS, and files can be opened from local storage. Tab-audio capture is desktop Chrome or Edge only. The interface is responsive and installable as an app.

Is it free?

Yes. It is an independent project with no ads, accounts or paid tier.
