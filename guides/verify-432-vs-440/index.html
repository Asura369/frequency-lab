How to verify 432 Hz vs 440 Hz

The difference is 31.8 cents — about a third of a semitone. It is measurable. What it means for how music feels is a different question, and not one a spectrum can answer.

What the numbers mean

Concert pitch fixes A4 at 440 Hz. “432 tuning” moves that reference down by 8 Hz. In cents — the logarithmic unit musicians use — the shift is 1200 · log₂(432 / 440) ≈ −31.8 cents. Every note in a retuned piece moves with the reference; it is not a single note dropped by 8 Hz.

This is why a note reader is useful: at 432 Hz the tool shows A4 −31.8 cents, not “A4, 8 Hz flat”. The cents value is the honest description of a tuning difference.

A procedure that produces a real answer

  1. Pick a sustained section. A pad, a drone, a held note or a reference tone. Skip drum fills and modulation.
  2. Set the target to 432 Hz with a tolerance of ±1 or ±2 Hz. The preset opens this for you.
  3. Play and watch the evidence for at least ten seconds. “Consistent” means a tone-like peak was in that band whenever there was signal.
  4. Read the peak table. If the track is tuned to 432, the strongest sustained line should sit at 432 (or its harmonics at 864, 1296 …), not 440.
  5. Repeat at 440 Hz. A properly retuned track should favour 432 and not 440. If both are strong, you may be looking at a chord, a layer, or a mix of sources.

What can go wrong

  • Vibrato and glides. A voice or string section moves by more than 8 Hz regularly. Use a steady passage, or accept “intermittent” as the correct verdict.
  • Chords. A chord contains many notes; none of them has to be A4. Test a single note or a drone, not a full harmony.
  • Resampling and transcoding. A 432 Hz file converted and re-encoded is still 432 Hz; platforms do not usually retune. But a speed change of 0.1% moves a note by about 1.7 cents, so check the source version.
  • A 432 Hz layer over 440 Hz music. Some “432 Hz” uploads are ordinary tracks with a quiet 432 Hz sine added. The tone is measurable; the claim that the music was retuned is not supported by it.
  • Speaker and room resonances. A room mode near 432 Hz can make that band look strong even when the source is not tuned there. Move the microphone or use a file, where the room is out of the loop.

What a good result looks like

A retuned reference tone gives a peak within a fraction of a hertz of 432, a clean note reading of A4 −31.8 cents, and a target verdict above 80% while the tone is playing. A retuned piece of music gives the same evidence during sustained passages, diluted by everything else that is happening.

Neither result says anything about relaxation, health or “natural” tuning. Those claims need controlled listening studies, not a spectrum. The tool is careful to stop at the acoustic fact.
